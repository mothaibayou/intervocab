TIK TOK and DOPAMINE

The Danger of Digital Addiction

🎧 Listen to the Story

"Just five minutes." That is what Tom told himself when he opened TikTok after dinner. He wanted to relax for a short time before doing his homework.

The first video showed a funny cat. The next one was about an amazing football goal. Then came a cooking video, a magic trick, and a man climbing a mountain. Every video was different, so Tom kept watching. When he finally looked at the clock, more than an hour had passed.

Many people have had the same experience. Apps like TikTok and YouTube Shorts are made to keep people watching. Every time you swipe, you may find something funny, exciting, or surprising. Because you never know what will come next, you want to keep looking. One more swipe might bring the best video yet.

Scientists say this happens because of a chemical in the brain called dopamine. Dopamine helps us feel interested and excited. When we see something new or fun, the brain releases a little dopamine. This makes us want to keep watching. After many short videos, the brain starts to expect this feeling again and again.

After a few months, Tom noticed a problem. Reading a book seemed boring. It was hard to study for more than fifteen minutes. Even watching a normal movie felt too slow. He picked up his phone whenever he had a free minute.

One weekend, Tom decided to take a break from TikTok. He deleted the app for one month.

The first week was not easy. Many times, he reached for his phone without thinking. Sometimes he even forgot that the app was gone. But after two weeks, something changed. He could read for longer. He finished his homework more quickly. He also enjoyed talking with his friends because he was not always looking at his phone.

Short videos are not always bad. They can teach us new things, make us laugh, and help us relax. The problem begins when they take too much of our time and attention.

Phones are useful tools, but we should be careful. It is better when we choose how to use our phones instead of letting our phones choose how to use us.
