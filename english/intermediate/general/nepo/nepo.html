Mina has worked for companies from different Asian backgrounds, and she eventually noticed something funny: the nationality changed, but the drama was surprisingly similar. At one company, she slowly discovered that some of her “coworkers” were actually the owner’s wife, mother-in-law, and other relatives. Then she found out something even better. There were two people actually doing the work… and five supervisors watching them. Five. For two people. As the Americans say, ‘too many chiefs and not enough Indians.’

And what were these five supervisors doing all day? Well… apparently not much actual supervising. There was plenty of gossip, micromanaging, office politics, and the occasional backstabbing. At another company, Mina discovered that the “board of directors” was basically a husband and wife. That was when she realized that “corporate structure” can sometimes just mean “family tree with job titles.” Years later, she found herself in another workplace with a familiar atmosphere: unhappy customers, good employees leaving, and certain people somehow remaining untouchable. Mina genuinely wanted to help make things better. But then she realized she was trying to fix a house while the people inside were busy arguing over who owned which room. There were little groups, alliances, power struggles, and enough office politics to qualify as a turf war. And somehow, Mina was just standing there thinking: “I came here to do my job. How did I end up in a political cockfighting ring?”

At this point, she has learned an important lesson: Sometimes the biggest problem in a company isn’t a lack of talented people. It’s having too many people who are untouchable. Through hard experience she learned that “Too many cooks spoil the stew”. Mina realized she needed to develop a new career skill: Professional Workplace Survival!
