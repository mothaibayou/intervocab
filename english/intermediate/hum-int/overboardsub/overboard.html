The William Brown Disaster: Survival, Sacrifice, and Necessity

🎧 Listen to the text

The story of the William Brown is a haunting account of survival and sacrifice. In 1841 the ship struck an iceberg near Newfoundland and began to sink. Panic erupted because there were not enough lifeboats. A crowded longboat carried survivors into a storm. Alexander Holmes helped row the leaking boat, but conditions became desperate. The crew decided to lighten the boat to prevent it from sinking. Several passengers were thrown into the icy sea. When the survivors reached Philadelphia, Holmes was arrested. His lawyers argued that he acted out of necessity. The jury disagreed and found him guilty, although he received a light sentence because of the extreme circumstances. Today the case is still studied because it raises difficult questions about fairness, responsibility, and survival.
